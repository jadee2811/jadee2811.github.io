AI in consumer electronics retail: practical use cases from Southeast Asia

A purchase decision in consumer electronics is increasingly a decision problem, and AI becomes valuable when it turns an ambiguous need into a specific decision.

A customer walks into an electronics store to buy a TV. But what they actually have to choose is not just a TV.

A decision problem, not a shopping problem

They have to navigate screen size, panel technology, operating systems, brands, price points, promotions, bundles and installation options. For laptops and smartphones, the trade-offs become even more complex.

In other words, a purchase decision in consumer electronics is increasingly becoming a decision problem, rather than simply a shopping problem.

And this is where AI becomes interesting. Not because AI can answer FAQs, but because it can sit between a huge amount of product intelligence and a customer trying to turn an ambiguous need into a specific decision.

Across Southeast Asia, consumer electronics retailers are already experimenting with this shift in different ways. Some are using AI to help customers discover products. Others are turning conversations into sales channels, augmenting sales teams, recovering missed leads, or personalizing what happens after the purchase.

The implementations look different. The underlying question is the same: where can AI make the customer journey more useful, while making the retail operation more scalable?

A typical electronics journey now looks like: Discover → Research → Compare → Consult → Purchase → Service → Upgrade

But the signals generated along the way are often fragmented across websites, apps, marketplaces, social channels, stores, CRM and customer service. The opportunity is to connect those signals into a more complete customer context: Customer signals → Context → AI → Next Best Action → Business Outcome

Looking across Southeast Asia, I see five practical use cases emerging around this journey.

1. AI product discovery

Customers often know what they need, but not which product they should search for. “I need a laptop for work, some gaming and travel” can lead to dozens of possible configurations.

Erajaya in Indonesia is using GenAI and semantic search to improve product discovery, while Shop Global in Thailand has developed an AI Personal Shopper that understands natural-language queries and contextual customer signals.

The shift is simple: traditional search matches keywords. AI discovery understands intent.

2. AI shopping advisor

Once customers find relevant products, they still need help deciding. This becomes particularly important when retailers have thousands of SKUs and highly technical specifications.

At MWG, Easy AI has supported more than 120,000 customers per month and around 25,000 messages per day, across a product universe of more than 100,000 products.

The value is not knowing every specification. It is knowing which product makes sense for this customer. This can directly influence conversion, AOV and time-to-decision.

3. Conversational commerce

Retail chat is moving from Question → Answer toward Need → Recommendation → Price → Promotion → Availability → Purchase

At Điện máy XANH, Easy AI reports more than 15,000 messages per day, supporting product, promotion, availability and service-related conversations. Senheng in Malaysia is taking a similar direction with AI-powered ChatCommerce, while Laptop88 in Vietnam has used AI to address fragmented conversations and missed leads across channels.

The opportunity is no longer simply to automate conversations. It is to turn conversations into a sales channel.

4. AI sales and service capacity

Consumer electronics requires expertise, but expert capacity does not scale indefinitely. AI can handle repetitive interactions while employees focus on more complex cases.

Pico in Vietnam, for example, reports 97% automated conversations and 94% customer satisfaction through its AI-native retail solution. The relevant question is therefore not “How much can we automate?” but “How much more value can each human hour create?”

This shifts the KPI from automation rate toward cost-to-serve, response time, CSAT and revenue per employee.

5. AI customer intelligence and next best action

The most strategic opportunity sits across the entire lifecycle. A customer may search for a TV, ask about installation, purchase it, later browse soundbars and eventually contact customer service. If these interactions remain disconnected, the retailer sees transactions. If they are connected, the retailer can understand intent and determine what should happen next.

Senheng uses AI for customer targeting, while Blibli in Indonesia applies AI to personalized customer journeys and recommendations.

This creates a continuous loop: Interaction → Signal → Customer Context → Recommendation → Action → New Signal. That is where a 360° customer view becomes commercially useful.

What these SEA cases tell us

Looking across the region, the implementations are different. MWG is dealing with enormous product and interaction complexity. Laptop88 is addressing fragmented channels and missed leads. Senheng is using AI to improve customer targeting and conversational commerce. Erajaya is improving search and conversational discovery. Shop Global is building an AI Personal Shopper around natural-language and contextual search. Pico is using AI to extend sales and service capacity. Blibli is connecting personalization with lifecycle engagement.

But the underlying pattern is remarkably consistent. Discover: AI helps customers find relevant products. Decide: AI helps them navigate complexity. Convert: AI turns conversations into commercial workflows. Serve: AI extends sales and service capacity. Grow: AI uses customer context to determine the next best action.

And underneath all of this sits the same foundation: a connected understanding of the customer. Without customer context, AI can answer. With customer context, AI can recommend. With product and commercial context, AI can act. And with the right workflow integration, those actions can become measurable business outcomes.

So where should consumer electronics retailers start?

I would not start with “Where can we use GenAI?”, I would start with “Where in the customer journey are we currently losing value?”. If the problem is product complexity, start with AI Product Discovery. If customers are struggling to choose, build an AI Shopping Advisor. If customer conversations are not converting, connect Conversational AI to the sales workflow. If service volume is growing faster than headcount, use AI to extend sales and service capacity. If customer data exists but rarely changes what the business does, build the CDP and AI layer needed for Next Best Action.

One journey. One use case. One measurable KPI. Then Pilot → Measure → Integrate → Scale.

The model is only one part of the equation: AI × Retail Data × Business Workflow × Human Expertise = Business Impact

Conclusion

Consumer electronics may be one of the clearest examples of where AI can move beyond automation. The products are complex. The decisions are high-consideration. The customer journey stretches across multiple channels and continues long after the transaction.

Across Southeast Asia, retailers are already building different pieces of the model. The next opportunity is to connect them. Because the goal is not to have an AI chatbot, an AI search engine or an AI copilot. It is to make every customer interaction more useful, every employee more effective, and every signal more actionable.

That is where AI starts becoming part of the revenue engine.
