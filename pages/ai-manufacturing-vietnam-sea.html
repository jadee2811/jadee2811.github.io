AI in manufacturing across Vietnam and SEA: why it's no longer optional, it's business survival

Manufacturing is shifting from "automated + manual" to "intelligent + adaptive", and AI is becoming a strategic imperative, not just a tool.

Across Vietnam and Southeast Asia, manufacturing companies are standing at a pivotal moment. For years, factories have invested in automation, lean processes, and incremental improvements. But today's environment (rapid tech innovation, rising quality demands, cost pressure, and global supply chain volatility) requires something more.

Automation alone is no longer enough

Manufacturers now need systems that can sense, learn, and adapt in real time. This is where AI becomes a strategic imperative, not just a tool. As someone who has implemented AI solutions across healthcare, retail, and manufacturing, I've seen the shift firsthand: companies that adopt AI early gain an edge; companies that delay fall behind.

This article kicks off a 3-part series exploring the market landscape, why AI is becoming a critical advantage, the real challenges manufacturers face, and what it takes to adopt AI successfully.

Vietnam's economic backbone, and a sector under pressure

Manufacturing holds enormous weight in Vietnam's economy. It contributes about 25% of national GDP, employs more than 12 million workers, and drives the majority of export value (electronics, machinery, textiles, components).

Across Southeast Asia, the momentum is even larger: ASEAN's manufacturing output is projected to grow from $1.7 trillion (2018) to $2.3 trillion by 2029. This growth has made Vietnam, Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand key manufacturing hubs for global giants such as Samsung, Foxconn, Intel, LG, Bosch, Panasonic, and others.

But with growth comes pressure. Manufacturers in Vietnam today face rising labor costs, higher global quality standards, shorter lead times and flexible customer demand, ESG and energy-saving requirements, and intense competition inside industrial parks. These pressures push factories past the limits of traditional workflows. The next leap requires AI-driven intelligence.

Why AI and smart manufacturing is now a strategic priority

National strategies are pushing AI adoption

Vietnam's National AI Strategy 2030 highlights AI, automation, and smart factories as national priorities. Government–enterprise programs are actively helping factories modernize and upgrade operations. When policies, market demand, and global competition all point in one direction, the question shifts from "Should we adopt AI?" to "How fast can we adopt it effectively?"

Clear, measurable business ROI

Smart manufacturing is one of the fastest-growing technology markets in Vietnam. Adoption is driven by use cases with fast ROI, including AI-powered quality inspection, predictive maintenance, inventory and supply chain optimization, production scheduling, and energy optimization. These aren't futuristic concepts. They're practical improvements that many Vietnamese factories are implementing today.

Real challenges manufacturers face when adopting AI

Even with strong market momentum, many factories still struggle to get started. Here are the biggest blockers I see in real projects.

Legacy equipment and lack of data

Around 70% of Vietnamese manufacturers are SMEs with aging machines, limited sensors, and fragmented data. Without clean, connected data, AI can't become effective.

Fragmented systems and inconsistent workflows

ERP, MES, SCADA, spreadsheets, manual logs: many factories operate with disconnected systems that don't talk to each other. This makes AI integration difficult and unpredictable.

Lack of hybrid talent (AI + operations)

Few people truly understand both how the production line works and how AI models and data pipelines work.

Misaligned expectations about cost and ROI

Some companies try to jump straight into full-factory digital twins, 100% robotic automation, or end-to-end AI deployments. These often require high investment and long lead time, which is too risky for most factories. Meanwhile, the manufacturers seeing success are the ones who start small, solve one high-impact problem, deliver ROI in 3-6 months, and scale gradually.

Cultural and change management barriers

Technology is the easy part. The hard part is changing habits, redesigning workflows, training teams, and aligning leadership. Factories need alignment before they can scale AI effectively.

The 3 principles for successful AI adoption in manufacturing

Based on my work with manufacturers, hospitals, and large retailers, I've found that success follows three core principles.

Principle 1: Start small. Prove ROI. Scale fast.

Begin with 1-2 high-impact, low-risk use cases: AI vision for defect detection, predictive maintenance for critical machines, inventory forecasting, energy optimization, or an AI chatbot for B2B sales or dealer support. Once proven, these early wins build momentum for larger transformations.

Principle 2: Build AI for both "factory operations" and "commercial operations"

A successful manufacturing company operates as one seamless chain. Inside the factory: quality inspection, machine health, production scheduling, warehouse and logistics. Outside the factory: product data accuracy, wholesale support, e-commerce, demand forecasting, customer support. This dual approach creates a reinforcing loop of efficiency and growth.

Principle 3: Build the data foundation early

You don't need complex infrastructure. Start with clean product and master data (BOM, materials, routing), machine performance logs, standardized processes and naming, and a single source of truth for inventory and operations. With this foundation, scaling AI becomes much easier, and far less risky.

Conclusion

AI isn't a trend. It's the next competitive advantage for manufacturers in Vietnam and SEA. Vietnam's manufacturing sector is entering a transformational era: labor advantages are shrinking, global competition is rising, customers demand speed, quality, and flexibility, ESG and energy efficiency are becoming mandatory, and FDI factories are already running AI-driven operations.

In this landscape, AI is no longer just "nice to have." It's a business differentiator, and soon, a basic requirement to stay in the game. From my experience working with leading enterprises, the winning strategy is clear:

Start small. Digitize what matters. Use AI to solve real problems. Then scale confidently.
